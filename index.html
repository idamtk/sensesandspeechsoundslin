How do different senses affect speech perception?

Thank you for your interest in the experiment! Read more about the experiment and whether you can participate below:

About the experiment

What is the experiment about?

The experiment is about what senses affect how we perceive speech. This will be investigated through an experiment where you listen to speech sounds (such as the syllables "sa" and "za") while you at the same time feel a mild vibration or tap on the hand. This can contribute to understanding how we perceive speech and how speech is processed in the brain.

Your participation in the experiment is voluntary. You can at any time before or during the experiment choose to withdraw from the experiment. If you regret your participation, you can withdraw your data up to three months after you have participated in the study. As a participant in the experiment you are anonymous. Your answers will be treated confidentially.

The experiment is 45-60 min long. As compensation for your participation in the study you course credit (if stipulated in the course syllabus, perhaps as part of a course engagement grade), or bonus course credit (i.e., 1-2% extra marks towards final grades). No credit will be awarded if the participants withdraw before completing the experiment.

The experiment has been approved by the Social Sciences, Humanities and Education Research Ethics Board at the University of Toronto (Protocol Reference #00051270)

The process of participating

How does it work?

  1. You answer the questions below to check whether you can participate in the experiment
  2. You can book an appointment for the experiment by emailing the provided email address.
  3. You show up to our lab for your experiment appointment
  4. You receive information about the study and decide whether to participate
  5. You will be asked to listen to some sounds and answer whether you have heard them or what you heard. Simultaneously with some of the sounds you will feel a mild vibration or a mild tap on the hand.
  6. You will be asked to answer a survey about your language background
  7. You will receive course credit as compensation for your particpation
